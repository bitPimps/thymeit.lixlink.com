Sources

Where the timings come from

Every care task in ThymeIt is tied to a quote from a university extension service or botanic garden, and your frost dates come from US government climate data.

How a timing gets into the app

For each care task, we keep a short quote from a published source (for example, “The late dormant season (late winter to early spring) is best for most pruning”) and a note on how we turned it into a window around your frost dates. A script re-checks every quote against the live page, so a source that changes gets noticed. Where a source times something by bloom or by when the ground freezes, the window is our estimate and is marked as one in our review notes.

These are general guidance for your area, not a guarantee for every season. A late cold snap or an early spring can move things; trust the weather in front of you.

Hardiness zones and frost dates

  • USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map (2023), by the USDA Agricultural Research Service (USDA-ARS) and the PRISM Group at Oregon State University. ThymeIt uses PRISM’s ZIP code zone tables, unaltered.
  • NOAA NCEI U.S. Climate Normals, 1991–2020: the median last spring and first fall 32°F freeze dates for the weather station nearest each ZIP code.
  • US Census Bureau ZCTA Gazetteer: ZIP code area locations, used to match a shared location to the nearest ZIP code.
